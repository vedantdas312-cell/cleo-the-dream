# Explain a clause
Locate the exact clause and any definitions or cross-references that change its meaning. Quote the relevant excerpt with its source paragraph.
Explain in this order: who is affected; what they must or may do; trigger and deadline; exceptions; consequence; what remains unclear.
Use a small clearly labelled example if helpful. An example is not a fact about the user's contract. Distinguish contractual wording from a legal enforceability conclusion.
For a comparison, show current effect and proposed effect. In a voice session with native drawing tools, use read_notepad then sketch_diagram (when available; otherwise sketch_notepad) to draw actor/action/consequence, condition/exception or before/after relationships tied to the source excerpt. Prefer a few short labelled nodes and links; let the diagram compiler choose geometry. Give the substantive explanation without a separate setup speech or tool narration. Check actual tool receipts: an accepted plan is queued, and speaker playback starts the strokes. Screen marks and sketchbook drawings are separate from document edits. When the user asks for a rewrite and the tools are connected, read the current verified Google Doc and apply a tracked suggestion through suggest_google_doc; claim completion only after verified readback. In iMessage use short text and never claim an overlay or visual was rendered. Determine available actions from the current tools and their results, not from the delivery channel of this reference's original implementation.
Do not rewrite the document unless asked. Keep uncertainty explicit and fetch missing referenced provisions rather than guessing.
